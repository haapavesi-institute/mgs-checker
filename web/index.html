MGS Checker

Counts every maximal green sequence of an acyclic quiver by length, exactly, and finds the maximal c-vectors its MGS pass through. It walks the oriented exchange graph one length deeper at a time, and stops when every green sequence has ended or when BDP's No Gap Conjecture says no longer MGS can exist. It always says which of the two happened. Quivers with an oriented cycle are refused. It runs entirely in your browser.

Conjecture #7: delete the multiple arrows

Quiver Quest conjecture #7: if no multiple arrow lies on an oriented cycle, every MGS of Q is an MGS of Q′, the quiver with every bundle of two or more arrows deleted, with the same c-vector at every step. For acyclic quivers (no oriented cycle; cycles of the underlying graph are fine) that is Igusa–Zhou's Theorem 3.12 (arXiv:1902.07375). The open part is quivers with an oriented cycle that avoids every multiple arrow: this section is the one place the checker accepts them.

Any quiver with a multiple arrow, oriented cycles allowed.

Method

  • Acyclic quivers only. A quiver with an oriented cycle is refused, and the refusal names a cycle; cycles of the underlying graph, as in 1 → 2 → 3 with 1 → 3, are fine. Explorations never add an arrow that would close an oriented cycle. The one exception is the Conjecture #7 section, whose open part needs oriented cycles.
  • Convention. Brüstle–Dupont–Pérotin framing: arrows i → i′ to frozen copies, and green means the c-vector is ≥ 0. Vertices are numbered from 1.
  • Counting. MGS are paths through the oriented exchange graph (BDP Prop. 2.13–2.14). States are memoised by their c-vectors, which determine the exchange matrix. The search checks this on every reuse. Counts are exact integers of any size.
  • Pruning. BHIT Cor. 3.3.2 is a theorem with no hypotheses: no MGS ever mutates a vertex that is the target of two or more parallel arrows. Those steps are skipped, which never loses an MGS.
  • Stopping. The search is exact with no assumption when every green sequence ends. It is exact by theorem when a No Gap theorem covers the quiver (tame: Hermes–Igusa, arXiv:1601.04054). Otherwise it is exact if the lengths of MGS have no gap longer than the chosen max gap (BDP Conjecture 2.22 is gap 0). Green sequences still running past that point are reported as empirically infinite.
  • Tentative bans. A tentatively banned prefix gets a second, unbounded search with a small budget. That search can prove the ban outright, when every continuation reaches a BHIT 3.3.2 step. It can also refute the gap assumption, by finding a longer MGS.
  • C-vectors. Each step of an MGS mutates a green vertex; its c-vector is a positive real Schur root. The check collects these step c-vectors over every MGS it counts and lists the maximal ones under the entrywise order. The order is partial, so several can be maximal. For Dynkin quivers they are all |Φ⁺| positive roots, and the only maximal one is the highest root. It also reports how many exchange-graph classes the MGS pass through, and the most parallel arrows met on the way.
  • Literature checks. Acyclic quivers: shortest length n (BDP 2.20). Dynkin: longest |Φ⁺| (BDP 4.4). Tame: longest by Kase–Nakashima (arXiv:2010.13996).
