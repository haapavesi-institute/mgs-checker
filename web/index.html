MGS Checker

Counts every maximal green sequence of a quiver by length, exactly. It walks the oriented exchange graph one length deeper at a time, and stops when every green sequence has ended or when BDP's No Gap Conjecture says no longer MGS can exist. It always says which of the two happened. It runs entirely in your browser.

Search

Max gap 0 is BDP's No Gap Conjecture: stop at the first empty length after the longest MGS found. A larger gap waits longer before concluding. For tame quivers, the No Gap theorem (Hermes–Igusa) makes the stop exact.

Method

  • Convention. Brüstle–Dupont–Pérotin framing: arrows i → i′ to frozen copies, and green means the c-vector is ≥ 0. Vertices are numbered from 1.
  • Counting. MGS are paths through the oriented exchange graph (BDP Prop. 2.13–2.14). States are memoised by their c-vectors, which determine the exchange matrix. The search checks this on every reuse. Counts are exact integers of any size.
  • Pruning. BHIT Cor. 3.3.2 is a theorem with no hypotheses: no MGS ever mutates a vertex that is the target of two or more parallel arrows. Those steps are skipped, which never loses an MGS.
  • Stopping. The search is exact with no assumption when every green sequence ends. It is exact by theorem when a No Gap theorem covers the quiver (tame: Hermes–Igusa, arXiv:1601.04054). Otherwise it is exact if the lengths of MGS have no gap longer than the chosen max gap (BDP Conjecture 2.22 is gap 0). Green sequences still running past that point are reported as empirically infinite.
  • Tentative bans. A tentatively banned prefix gets a second, unbounded search with a small budget. That search can prove the ban outright, when every continuation reaches a BHIT 3.3.2 step. It can also refute the gap assumption, by finding a longer MGS.
  • Literature checks. Acyclic quivers: shortest length n (BDP 2.20). Dynkin: longest |Φ⁺| (BDP 4.4). Tame: longest by Kase–Nakashima (arXiv:2010.13996).
